How I think

The first answer is rarely the root cause

Eight investigations taught me to keep asking. Walk through a representative calibration deviation, one why at a time.

Deviation investigation · 5-Why

DEV-REP-001 · INTERACTIVE METHOD DEMONSTRATION

Deviation as reported An out-of-tolerance result was found on a balance used for in-process weight checks — discovered three days after the scheduled verification.
  1. The balance failed its scheduled calibration verification.
  2. Verification ran on schedule, but the out-of-tolerance result wasn't escalated the same day.
  3. The escalation step lives in a separate SOP that the daily log doesn't reference.
  4. That cross-reference was dropped when the daily log SOP was last revised.
  5. Change control review didn't include an impact assessment on linked documents.
0 / 5

Root cause

The change control process had no mandatory impact assessment for linked documents, so a routine revision silently removed an escalation path. The balance was the symptom; the document system was the cause.

Correction

Quarantine affected batches, re-verify in-process weights against retained records, requalify the balance.

Corrective action

Add a mandatory linked-document impact assessment to the change control form, routed through QA.

Preventive action

Add a cross-reference check to periodic SOP review; retrain document owners and log the training.

Effectiveness check: review the next three change controls and audit 30 days of daily logs for same-day escalation compliance.
